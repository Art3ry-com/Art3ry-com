License display is not optional

California requires real estate licensees to disclose their license identification number on solicitation materials intended to be the first point of contact with consumers. The Department of Real Estate's rule under Business and Professions Code section 10140.6 covers things like business cards, flyers, mail and internet advertising, with some exceptions such as certain print ads and "For Sale" signs. The type size cannot be smaller than the smallest type used in the material.

On a site, that means your DRE number belongs in the header or footer, and on every lead-gen page or flyer. Confirm current requirements with the DRE for your specific materials.

What portals cannot copy

Portals have the listings and the budget. They do not have your memory of the street, your read on which blocks sell fast, or your relationships with the people who live there. A good local page makes that knowledge searchable, in plain language, with specifics a portal would never publish.

That is also why I do not write these pages for you from scratch. I structure what you know, check it for consistency and make sure it is readable to Google. A neighborhood page written from someone's actual experience sounds different from one assembled from public data, and readers can tell.

Consistency across every listing

Your name, brokerage, phone and license number should read identically on your site, your profile and every directory. Small inconsistencies split your signals and make you look less established than you are. Cleaning them up is a quiet first step with a real payoff.
