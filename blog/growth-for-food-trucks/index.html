Food Truck Growth: Get Found, Get Booked, Fill the Catering Calendar

Service stops pay the bills. Catering builds the business. Both depend on being findable and fast to answer. Here is how a truck handles that while the grill is hot.

Two businesses on one truck

A food truck is really two businesses. The first sells to whoever walks up today: volume, thin margin, weather-dependent. The second sells to someone planning an event: a date, a headcount, a budget, and a much bigger ticket. Catering usually decides whether a truck stays a hustle or becomes a business.

The catch is that the two run on different clocks. Service stops happen at noon, when you cannot touch your phone. Catering inquiries arrive at 10:30 at night, when you are cleaning up or asleep. The inquiry goes unanswered, and the planner books whoever replied.

I run a one-person California field-services company, and 700+ jobs have gone through a system I built to answer, collect details, and follow up on a schedule that is not mine. The same approach fits a truck.

Part one: be findable where you are today

Customers do not search for a taco truck by name. They search for tacos near me, or food trucks in a specific part of town. Your Google Business Profile is the answer to that, and it needs to be accurate. Update your hours and location when they change, add real photos of the food and the truck, and pick the exact category.

Google lists relevance, distance, and prominence as the factors behind local results. A moving truck cannot win on distance, so lean on the other two: a complete profile and fresh reviews. The mechanics are in how to rank on Google Maps.

Post your location where your regulars look, at the same time every day. Consistency matters more than polish. The followers who know where you will be at 11 AM are the ones who show up.

Part two: answer the catering inquiry like it is a customer

A catering inquiry deserves an answer within minutes, even if the answer is a short acknowledgment and a few questions. Date, headcount, location, budget range, dietary needs, and whether they need serving staff. Collect those up front and you can price the job without a call.

Speed matters because the planner is comparing trucks and picks the one who makes it easy. A Harvard Business Review study of 1.25 million leads found that contacting a lead within an hour made it nearly seven times as likely to be qualified as waiting even an hour longer. It is not a food truck study, but the lesson fits an event planner exactly.

Put a clear catering page on your site or profile with sample menus, minimums, and the area you cover. That page answers half the questions before anyone asks.

Part three: quote, deposit, and confirm

Send the quote the same day, with the menu, price, and a link to pay a deposit. A deposit blocks the date on your calendar and filters out the casually curious. Confirm details a few days before the event: address, load-in time, power or parking needs, and a contact on site.

If they go quiet after the quote, follow up. Three short messages over two weeks win jobs that were never lost. The timing and wording are in how to follow up on a quote.

Part four: turn events into the next event

A catered event puts your food in front of fifty people who all know someone planning something. Leave cards or a QR code. Send the host a thank-you with a review link the next day. Ask permission to use a photo from the event. One good catering job should seed the next two.

Do not offer a discount in return for a review; Google prohibits incentivized reviews. A direct link and a sincere ask are allowed and work. See how to get more Google reviews for the full method.

California notes

Permits, commissary requirements, and county health rules vary across California, so check the requirements for each county you work in and put your permit information on your catering page. Customers planning an event want to know you are legitimate. In the Valley, heat shapes the season: summer events cluster on evenings and weekends, so quote early and block your calendar accordingly.

Start small

Fix your profile, build a one-page catering menu, and set up a reply that goes out the moment an inquiry arrives. Then add the deposit link and the follow-up. FAQ

How do food trucks get catering bookings?
Make inquiries easy and answer them within minutes, collecting date, headcount, location, and budget. Have a clear catering page, send a quote the same day with a deposit link, and follow up if they go quiet.
How do I get my food truck found on Google?
Claim and complete your Google Business Profile, keep hours and location accurate, add real photos, choose the right category, and collect recent reviews.
Should I take deposits for catering?
Yes. A deposit secures the date on your calendar and filters out casual inquiries. Send the payment link with the quote.
How often should I post my location?
On the same schedule every day, ideally before lunch. Regulars rely on consistency more than on polish.
Can automation handle food truck inquiries?
It can acknowledge inquiries instantly, collect event details, send quotes and deposit links, and follow up. Menu decisions and special requests are best left to you.
