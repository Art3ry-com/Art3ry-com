An AI Assistant for Busy Parents: Your Family's Back Office, Handled

Running a family is running a small operation. You're the one doing all the jobs.

The job nobody put on your calendar

Somewhere along the way you became your family's operations manager. You hold the schedules, the appointments, the permission slips, the who-needs-to-be-where-and-when. It's a full workload, and it's invisible, and it never clocks out.

The hard part isn't any single task. It's the sheer volume of small things that all live in your head at once — the dentist appointment, the field trip form, the reminder to reschedule the thing you missed because you were handling the other thing.

It's a back office. Yours just happens to be a household. And like any back office run by one overwhelmed person, things slip through — and the slips cost you stress, money, and time.

What a family back office assistant handles

ART3RY is an assistant you talk to in plain language, connected to your email and calendar — which, for most families, is exactly where the chaos lives. The appointment confirmations, the school emails, the scheduling, the date you keep meaning to put down.

You don't manage an app. You just say what you need the way you'd ask a trusted helper: get this on the calendar, remind me about that, draft the reply to the coach. It pulls the context from your inbox and calendar and gets it ready.

Overwhelmed in the school pickup line? Call your assistant. It already knows what's on the calendar and what's hanging over you. That's the whole idea — relief in plain words, not another tool to babysit.

Your family's life is private — and stays in your hands

When it comes to your family, the stakes feel different than business. The thought of an automated system emailing your kid's teacher or messaging another parent on its own is a non-starter, and rightly so.

ART3RY is built for exactly this comfort level. It does the preparing and organizing, but anything that sends a message on your behalf, charges a card, or commits you to a customer waits for your one-word YES on your own phone. Nothing reaches another human until you say go.

So you get the help of an assistant without ever handing over the wheel on the things that involve your family's name, your money, or your relationships.

Powerful because it's safe — that's the point for parents

It would be easy to build something that just blasts out reminders and replies automatically. That would also be exactly the thing a parent can't trust. The value here isn't raw automation. It's automation you'd actually let near your family.

Every action with a real-world consequence pauses for your approval. That single rule is what makes it usable for the most personal back office there is. You stay the parent in charge; the assistant just carries the load you'd otherwise carry alone.

The result is fewer dropped balls and a quieter head, without trading away the control that, for a family, you'd never want to give up.

If a system can be trusted to keep a real business's commitments straight while never sending or charging anything without a yes, it can be trusted with the school calendar and the family logistics.
