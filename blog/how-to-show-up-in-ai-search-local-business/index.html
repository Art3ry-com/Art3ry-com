How to Show Up in AI Search as a Local Business

Customers now ask a chatbot who to hire. Nobody has a proven playbook for it yet, but the basics are not a mystery. Here is what Google says, what is guesswork, and what I would do first.

People are asking a machine who to hire

A homeowner types a question into an AI assistant: who is a good electrician near Visalia that can do a panel upgrade? Instead of ten blue links, they get a paragraph and a short list of names. If yours is not on it, you were not in the room.

That is a new door, and a lot of people are selling keys to it. Most of what gets sold is confident and unproven. I would rather tell you what is known, what is not, and what is worth doing regardless of how the technology moves.

I run a one-person California field-services company, and 700+ jobs have gone through the system I built. When I pointed that system at my own site, organic traffic rose 60% in 28 days with no ad spend. I do not have a secret trick for chatbots. I have a habit of doing the unglamorous things that every search system rewards.

The principle: AI search reads what regular search reads

Here is the most useful sentence in this whole space, and it comes from Google. On appearing in AI Overviews and AI Mode, [Google says there are no additional requirements and no special optimizations necessary, and that SEO best practices remain relevant](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).

Read that twice, because it cuts through the noise. The thing that gets you into the AI answer is mostly the thing that gets you into the normal results: pages that can be crawled, clear and useful content, accurate business information, and signs that real people trust you.

I want to be careful about scope. That statement is about Google's own AI features. Other assistants pull from different sources and Google does not speak for them. So treat everything below as the habits most likely to help across the board, not a guarantee for any one product.

Why this matters more for local businesses than you think

AI answers favor sources that look like a settled consensus. If your name, services, and city appear the same way on your site, your Google profile, and a handful of other trusted places, a system has an easy time deciding you exist and what you do. If those disagree, you become a risk, and a model that wants to give a safe answer will skip you.

There is also a speed effect. When an assistant gives one short list instead of a page of results, the gap between first place and fifth place gets wider. Being in the top few matters more than it did. That is a good reason to fix the basics now, while your competitors are still arguing about acronyms.

How to apply it, part one: make the facts match everywhere

Choose the exact way you write your business name, phone, address or service area, and hours. Use it identically on your website, your Google Business Profile, your Facebook page, and any directory that matters to your trade. Small differences like Ste versus Suite do less harm than a different phone number or a wrong city, but fix them anyway.

Keep your profile current. [Google specifically lists keeping Business Profile information up to date as part of appearing well in its AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features). Holiday hours, new services, a changed number: update them the day they change. The checklist for that is in Google Business Profile optimization.

Part two: answer the actual questions in plain text

An assistant can only quote what is written on a page. Put the real answers in readable text: what you do, what areas you cover, what a typical job involves, how long it takes, how you handle emergencies, and what a customer should have ready when they call. Google also recommends presenting important content in text form rather than burying it in images.

Write headings that sound like questions people ask. Do you repair tankless water heaters? Do you serve Madera County? Then answer in the first sentence under the heading, and add detail below. A short FAQ section on each service page does this naturally. This is the same method I use on every post here, including why your business is not showing up on Google.

Say specific things only you can say. A description of a job you did last month in a named neighborhood is something a model cannot find on fifty other sites, and it is the closest thing to a moat a small business has.

Part three: add structured data that matches the page

Structured data is a labeled version of your facts for machines. [Google's LocalBusiness guidance requires a name and address and recommends adding hours, phone, and location coordinates where you can](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business). Add FAQ and service details that reflect what is visible on the page, and only that.

Google also says to align structured data with visible content, which is the rule that keeps this honest. If the markup says you are open on Sundays and the page says you are closed, you have created the kind of conflict that makes systems trust you less. If you use a site builder, check whether it already outputs this before you add anything. If you would rather not touch markup yourself, that is part of what a website build should handle.

Part four: earn mentions from real places

Reviews, local news, the chamber of commerce, supplier directories, trade associations, and the occasional guest post are all places where other people vouch for you. Reviews especially: a steady flow of recent, specific ones is the strongest trust signal a local business has, and I covered how to get them in how to get more Google reviews.

Do not buy mentions or manufacture them. They are cheap to fake and easy to spot, and the penalty falls on you.

What is unproven, so you can ignore the hype

Nobody outside the companies running these systems can tell you exactly how a given assistant decides whom to recommend, and it changes. Anyone selling a guaranteed ranking inside a chatbot is guessing, or selling. There is also no reliable way yet to measure how often you are mentioned across all of them.

What you can measure: ask a few assistants the question your customer would ask, once a month, and note whether you appear. Watch Search Console for impressions on question-style searches. Ask new customers how they found you, and add a line for AI to the list. That is crude, and it is honest.

What I would do this month

Fix the facts so they match everywhere. Add a real FAQ to your top three service pages. Check your structured data. Ask your last ten happy customers for reviews. Then repeat the same small habits every month, because consistency is what compounds in search.

FAQ

How do I get my business to show up in ChatGPT?
There is no guaranteed method. The habits most likely to help are consistent business information everywhere, clear text answers on your site, accurate structured data, and a steady flow of real reviews and mentions. Check by asking assistants the question your customers would ask.
Do I need special optimization for Google AI Overviews?
Google says there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that standard SEO best practices remain relevant. Crawlable pages, useful content, and current Business Profile information are the foundation.
Does structured data help with AI search?
It helps machines understand your facts, and Google recommends aligning structured data with visible page content. It is not a shortcut. Use LocalBusiness markup with your real name, address, hours, and phone, and keep it consistent with the page.
Is SEO still worth it if AI answers questions directly?
Yes. The same fundamentals feed both, and Google says clicks from pages with AI Overviews tend to be higher quality engagement. Ranking well in normal search remains the base that AI features draw from.
How can I tell if AI assistants recommend my business?
Once a month, ask a few assistants the question your customer would type, such as best electrician near you, and note whether you appear. Also add AI search as an option when you ask new customers how they found you.
