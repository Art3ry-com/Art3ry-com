SEO for Contractors and Plumbers: How to Get the Calls Instead of the Directory

Most contractor SEO advice is written for agencies. This is the short version for someone with a truck, a crew and no time: what to fix first, what to skip, and why the call matters as much as the ranking.

Where contractor customers actually come from

Someone's water heater fails, a breaker keeps tripping, the AC quits in July. They pick up a phone and search a short phrase with a city in it, then call one of the first few businesses they see. Those first few are usually the map results, not the website results underneath.

That is why SEO for contractors and SEO for plumbers are mostly local SEO. You are not trying to rank for the whole country. You are trying to be one of three names on a map and a list for the towns you actually drive to.

Step one: the Google Business Profile

Start here because it moves the map results fastest and costs nothing but time. Claim and verify the profile, pick the one primary category that matches your main money job (not the broadest one), and add the secondary categories you really do. A plumber who also does water heaters and drain cleaning should say so in the categories and services, not leave it blank.

Fill in every field: hours, service area, phone, website, photos of real work and your real truck. Keep the business name exactly what is on your sign and invoice. Stuffing keywords into the name is against Google's rules and is a common reason profiles get suspended. If your profile is missing or stuck, work through why a business is not showing up on Google before anything else.

Step two: one page per service and per town

A single Services page cannot rank for water heater repair, drain cleaning, panel upgrades and AC repair in six towns. Search engines match a page to a search, so give each job you want its own page, and give your biggest towns their own page too, with real content: the work you do there, the neighborhoods, a photo or two, the number to call.

The trap is copying the same page and swapping the city name. Pages like that tend to get ignored. Write each one so it would be useful to a homeowner in that town on its own. I cover the method in service area pages that rank.

Step three: reviews, asked for on the day

Reviews influence both the map ranking and whether a stranger picks you. The reliable method is boring: ask the same day, by text, with a direct link, while the customer is still happy about the fix. Waiting a week cuts the response rate sharply. Reply to every review, including the bad ones, calmly and without arguing. There is a full walkthrough in how to get more Google reviews.

The part SEO advice skips: the call itself

You can win the ranking and still lose the job. A contractor on a ladder or under a sink cannot answer, the call goes to voicemail, and the caller moves down the list to the next number. Plenty of people will not leave a message for a trade they have never used.

So the ranking work only pays if the phone is answered. That means either a person who picks up or an assistant that answers, takes the details and texts them to you. If you want the numbers on that, see missed call lead capture for the trades and what an AI receptionist is.

What to ignore

Skip buying links from vendors who email you, skip posting the same text to dozens of directories you have never heard of, and skip any promise of a guaranteed number one ranking. Nobody outside Google can guarantee that. A correct profile, real service pages, steady reviews and a phone that gets answered will do more than any of it.

FAQ

How long does SEO take for a contractor?
Profile and review fixes can move map results within weeks. New service and town pages usually take a few months to settle. Nobody can promise an exact timeline, because it depends on your area and your competitors.
Do I need a website to show up on Google Maps?
A verified Google Business Profile can appear without one, but a site with real service and town pages gives you far more searches to appear for and gives callers a reason to trust you.
Is SEO or Google Ads better for a plumber?
Ads buy calls today and stop when you stop paying. SEO takes longer and keeps working. Many contractors use ads to cover the gap while the profile and pages build.
What is the first thing I should fix?
Your Google Business Profile: verified, the right primary category, accurate hours and service area, and real photos. Then make sure the phone gets answered.
